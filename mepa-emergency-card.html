Crisis Algorithms — Airway / Respiratory

MEPA / SIMPACT · Commonwealth guidelines (APA · DAS · AAGBI · RCUK)
Declare the emergency early · assign roles
Call for help before you need it.

Non-technical skills — scored in every scenario

  • Declare the emergency out loud & name it
  • Call for help early — senior + runner
  • Allocate roles: airway, drugs, CPR, scribe
  • Closed-loop communication for every drug
  • Think aloud — share the mental model
  • Re-evaluate after each intervention

Laryngospasm

Airway
  1. Call for help; remove the stimulus (secretions, airway, surgery).
  2. 100% O₂ + CPAP via tight-fitting mask, close APL valve.
  3. Larson's manoeuvre — firm pressure at the laryngospasm notch behind ear lobes, jaw thrust.
  1. Deepen anaesthesia: Propofol 2–3 mg/kg IV.
  2. If unbroken / desaturating: Suxamethonium 1–2 mg/kg IV (4 mg/kg IM if no access) + Atropine 20 mcg/kg.
  3. Ventilate, intubate if needed; watch for negative-pressure pulmonary oedema.
Bradycardia in a hypoxic child = Pre-arrest!!! — Treat the hypoxia

Bronchospasm

Airway
  1. 100% O₂; exclude mechanical causes — tube position, kink, blockage, breathing circuit.
  2. Deepen anaesthesia (volatile / Propofol); consider Ketamine.
  3. Salbutamol — MDI via circuit or neb 2.5–5 mg; IV 15 mcg/kg slow.
  1. Severe / silent chest: Adrenaline 1 mcg/kg IV titrated, or IM.
  2. Add Magnesium 40 mg/kg IV, Hydrocortisone, Ipratropium.
  3. Hand-ventilate — feel compliance; allow long expiratory time (avoid gas trapping).

Inhaled Foreign Body / Rigid Bronchoscopy

Airway · Shared airway
  1. Don't convert a partial obstruction to complete. Keep the child calm, upright, undisturbed; senior anaesthetist + ENT + theatre before induction.
  2. Maintain spontaneous ventilation — inhalational (sevoflurane) induction in 100% O₂, no paralysis until the airway is controlled and the surgeon is scrubbed.
  3. Deepen adequately; topicalise the cords (Lidocaine, max 3 mg/kg) to blunt laryngospasm before instrumentation.
  1. Shared airway: ventilate through the rigid bronchoscope side-arm; communicate continuously with the surgeon; TIVA helps maintain depth during instrumentation.
  2. Watch for: complete obstruction if the FB migrates, ball-valve air trapping, desaturation, laryngospasm on emergence, post-op subglottic oedema (Dexamethasone, nebulised Adrenaline).
  3. If total obstruction & cannot ventilate → push the FB distally into a main bronchus to ventilate the other lung, then retrieve.
Spontaneous ventilation + inhalational technique is the safe default — paralysing before the airway is secured can lose it entirely.

Difficult Airway Pathways

MEPA / SIMPACT · APA / DAS paediatric algorithms (1–8 yr)
Give 100% O₂ & call for help at step one, every pathway
Maintain spontaneous ventilation until the airway is secure
Don't repeat what just failed — change something.

NAP4 lessons — why paediatric airways go wrong

  • Poor assessment & failure to plan for failure
  • Repeated intubation attempts → oedema → CICO
  • Slow response to hypoxia → bradycardia → arrest
  • Failure to use the LMA as a rescue
  • Missing monitoring — SpO₂ + capnography
  • Desaturation is faster in children (low FRC)

Unanticipated Difficult Mask Ventilation

Airway · APA/DAS Fig 1
  1. 100% O₂ + call for help. Is this equipment or patient? Check mask, circuit, O₂ supply — have a self-inflating bag ready.
  2. Optimise head position — neutral in a child; shoulder roll if <6 months. Add/remove the roll as needed.
  3. Airway-opening manoeuvres: chin lift, jaw thrust; apply PEEP/CPAP; adjust or release cricoid pressure if used.
  4. Insert an oropharyngeal airway (if deep enough); deepen anaesthesia — exclude laryngospasm.
  1. Two-person technique: one holds mask with both hands, the other bags. Avoid pressing soft tissue with mask fingers.
  2. Decompress the stomach (NG tube) — gastric inflation is common in infants and splints the diaphragm.
  3. Still impossible / SpO₂ falling → insert an LMA (first choice).
  4. OR deepen, visualise cords, intubate. If LMA fails → move to the CICO pathway. If ventilating well: proceed or wake — if in doubt, wake the child.
Functional causes (light anaesthesia, laryngospasm, gastric inflation) are commoner than anatomical — deepen and decompress early.

Cannot Ventilate via LMA

Airway · rescue
  1. 100% O₂ + call for help. The LMA is your rescue device — optimise it before abandoning it.
  2. Reseat / resize: check depth (light → laryngospasm), reposition head (sniffing), jaw thrust, check cuff. Try up/down a size.
  3. Exclude patient causes: laryngospasm (deepen: Propofol), bronchospasm, gastric distension (NG/OG), breath-stacking (disconnect).
  1. 2nd-gen LMA: pass a gastric tube via the drain channel to decompress the stomach & improve seal.
  2. Still failing: remove LMA → face-mask (two-person) or intubate (LMA as conduit).
  3. Mask + LMA + intubation all fail & SpO₂ falling → declare CICO → front-of-neck access.
LMA failing to ventilate is a step toward CICO — optimise fast, don't fixate.

Unanticipated Difficult Tracheal Intubation

Airway · APA/DAS Fig 2
  1. 100% O₂ + call for help. Maintain oxygenation between every attempt — mask-ventilate to recover SpO₂.
  2. Limit to 3–4 attempts total. Each attempt must change something — do not repeat the identical technique.
  3. Change personnel (most experienced anaesthetist), position, or equipment.
  1. Optimise the view: external laryngeal manipulation, adequate depth & paralysis, bougie or stylet, straight blade (infants), alternate/video laryngoscope.
  2. Larynx impossible to view → insert an LMA (oxygenate; may serve as a conduit for fibreoptic intubation).
  3. LMA fails to ventilate → CICO pathway. Ventilating adequately but not intubated: wake the child or proceed on the LMA.
Repeated laryngoscopy causes airway oedema that converts a difficult airway into a CICO — a change of approach, not more attempts.

Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate (CICO)

Airway · APA/DAS Fig 3
  1. Declare CICO. Confirm all mask/LMA steps have failed. Call for the most senior help + ENT/surgeon and the difficult-airway trolley.
  2. If a reversible relaxant was used (Rocuronium/Vecuronium) and waking is an option → Sugammadex and wake the child.
  3. Act when SpO₂ <80% and falling and/or HR dropping. The critical failure is doing nothing — someone must attempt a technique.
  1. Front-of-neck access — choose by age/anatomy:
    Older / larger child → scalpel cricothyroidotomy (adult evidence favours surgical; recruit the surgeon).
    Infant / small child → the cricothyroid membrane (~2.6 × 3 mm) is smaller than any tube — a surgical tracheostomy or inter-ring approach by ENT is often more appropriate.
  2. Cannula technique: extend neck, stabilise trachea, insert cannula, confirm air aspiration, oxygenate via a purpose-made device. Beware barotrauma & the flaccid, mobile paediatric trachea.
  3. Scalpel technique: extend neck → stab through skin + cricothyroid membrane → tracheal hook at lower edge → railroad tube → ventilate, confirm ETCO₂, secure.
Infant anatomy is the trap — CTM barely exists; locating between tracheal rings is easier.
Get ENT if time allows, but never delay the only technique that oxygenates.

Paediatric Cardiac Arrest (APLS / RCUK)

Circulation · RCUK
  1. Confirm arrest; call resus team; start CPR 15:2 (2 rescuers), rate 100–120, depth ⅓ chest.
  2. Attach defib/monitor; rhythm check — shockable vs non-shockable.
  3. Non-shockable (PEA/asystole — the common paediatric arrest): Adrenaline 10 mcg/kg IV/IO immediately, then every 3–5 min.
  1. Shockable (VF/pVT): 4 J/kg shock; Adrenaline + Amiodarone 5 mg/kg after 3rd shock (repeat after 5th).
  2. Airway: ventilate 100% O₂; once advanced airway in, continuous compressions + 25 breaths/min.
  3. Reversible causes — 4 H & 4 T: Hypoxia, Hypovolaemia, Hypo/hyperkalaemia & metabolic, Hypothermia · Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins, Thromboembolism.
In children, hypoxia & hypovolaemia are the usual precipitants — FIX AIRWAY AND VOLUME FIRST

Acute Hypotension

AAGBI QRH · circulation
  1. Confirm it's real: recheck cuff size/position, transducer level/zero, arterial trace. Palpate a central pulse. Give 100% O₂.
  2. Immediate support: IV fluid bolus 10 mL/kg (repeat to 20 mL/kg); titrated vasopressor bolus (Ephedrine or Adrenaline) while finding the cause.
  3. Reduce obvious causes: lighten if too deep; check high intrathoracic pressure (gas trapping, tension, high PEEP) — disconnect & hand-ventilate to test.
  1. Differential: hypovolaemia / haemorrhage, anaphylaxis (rash, bronchospasm, ↑airway pressure), arrhythmia, LAST, sepsis, embolism (air/PE), cardiac (ischaemia, tamponade), high neuraxial block.
  2. Surgical causes: caval compression, vagal/peritoneal traction, laparoscopic gas embolism, major blood loss — talk to the surgeon.
  3. Treat the cause; escalate; arterial line, invasive monitoring, inotrope/vasopressor infusion if not responding.
Severe, sudden hypotension with high airway pressure or a rash — treat as anaphylaxis until proven otherwise.

SVT

Circulation · RCUK
  1. Assess stability: conscious level, perfusion, BP.
  2. Stable: vagal manoeuvres (ice to face in infants; Valsalva).
  3. Adenosine 100–150 mcg/kg rapid IV + flush; ↑ by 50–100 mcg/kg every 1–2 min.
  1. Unstable (shock, ↓consciousness): synchronised DC 1 J/kg → 2 J/kg.
  2. Seek expert help; treat reversible causes; consider Amiodarone.

Anaphylaxis

Circulation · RCUK
  1. STOP the likely trigger (antibiotic, NMBA, latex, chlorhexidine, colloid).
  2. Call for help; declare anaphylaxis; 100% O₂, secure airway.
  3. Adrenaline IM by age band — repeat every 5 min. In theatre with monitoring & access, titrated IV 1 mcg/kg boluses by an experienced anaesthetist.
  4. IV fluid bolus 20 mL/kg crystalloid, repeat as needed — vasodilatation is profound.
  1. Refractory (≥2 IM doses): start Adrenaline infusion 0.05–0.3 mcg/kg/min; consider Vasopressin, Glucagon (if β-blocked).
  2. Second line (after stabilisation): Hydrocortisone, Chlorphenamine by age.
  3. Investigations: mast-cell tryptase — take at diagnosis, 1–2 h, and 24 h.
  4. Document; refer for allergy testing; report the reaction.
Grade by severity · IM Adrenaline is first-line & lifesaving — Don't delay for IV access.

Air / Gas (Venous) Embolism

Circulation
  1. Suspect: sudden fall in ETCO₂, desaturation, hypotension, arrhythmia — neurosurgery (sitting), laparoscopy, CVC, open venous surgery.
  2. Stop entrainment: flood field with saline, pack wound, surgeon occludes source; release pneumoperitoneum.
  3. 100% O₂, stop N₂O. Call for help.
  1. Position: head-down + left lateral (Durant) — traps gas in RV apex.
  2. Aspirate from a CVC if in situ. Support circulation: fluids 20 mL/kg, Ephedrine or Adrenaline; CPR if arrest.
  3. Hyperbaric O₂ if paradoxical / cerebral embolism; watch for pulmonary oedema; ICU.
Sudden ETCO₂ fall + hypotension in a high-risk case = embolism until proven otherwise.

Malignant Hyperthermia

Metabolic · AAGBI / RXH
  1. STOP all volatile / Suxamethonium; change circuit / use wall O₂; call for help + declare MH; get the MH kit.
  2. 100% O₂ high flow; hyperventilate; convert to TIVA; remove vaporisers.
  3. Dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg IV bolus, repeat every 5 min until signs reverse → up to 30 mg/kg. (20 mg vials, each in 60 mL sterile water — assign a mixer.)
  4. Active cooling if T>39 °C — cold IV fluids, surface ice, cavity lavage; stop at 38 °C; avoid vasoconstriction.
  1. Hyperkalaemia: Ca gluconate 0.5 mL/kg, glucose–insulin, NaHCO₃ (see hyperK panel).
  2. Arrhythmias: Magnesium / Amiodarone. Do NOT use Ca-channel blockers — hyperkalaemia & arrest with Dantrolene.
  3. Acidosis: NaHCO₃ 1 mL/kg if gas unavailable, else BE-based. Catheterise, aim urine >0.5 mL/kg/h; if low → Furosemide 1 mg/kg or Mannitol 0.25 g/kg.
  4. Monitor: core temp, ETCO₂, ABG, K⁺, CK, myoglobin, coagulation. ICU + continue Dantrolene.
Triad: ↑ETCO₂, Tachycardia, Rigidity / Masseter spasm — with Acidosis, ↑Temp (late), Myoglobinuria

Anaesthesia-Induced Rhabdomyolysis (AIR)

Metabolic · RXH
  1. Recognise — this is NOT MH: presents with bradycardia, hyperkalaemia, peaked T-waves, cardiac arrest (often in an undiagnosed myopathy after Suxamethonium/volatile). No Dantrolene.
  2. STOP all triggers (volatile + Suxamethonium); change machine / use wall O₂; call for help.
  3. 100% O₂ + hyperventilate. ABG — check K⁺.
  1. Treat hyperkalaemia once confirmed (see hyperK panel) — this is the priority.
  2. Catheterise, aim urine >1 mL/kg/h: IV fluid boluses + Mannitol 0.25 g/kg.
  3. If pulseless or HR <60 → start CPR. CPR >20 min → consider bypass / ECMO. Send blood + urine myoglobin, CK.
Key distinction: AIR = Hyperkalaemic arrest (treat the K⁺); MH = Hypermetabolic (give Dantrolene). Don't confuse them.

LA Systemic Toxicity

Neuro · AAGBI
  1. STOP injecting LA; call for help; 100% O₂, secure airway.
  2. Control seizures: Benzodiazepine / small Propofol (avoid large Propofol if unstable).
  3. Intralipid 20% 1.5 mL/kg bolus over 1 min, then infusion 15 mL/kg/h.
  1. Repeat bolus ×2 every 5 min if unstable; double infusion rate. Max ≈ 12 mL/kg.
  2. If arrest: prolonged CPR (>1 h); small Adrenaline doses (<1 mcg/kg); avoid Vasopressin, Ca-blockers, LA.

Hyperkalaemia Management

Metabolic · RXH / APLS
  1. Confirm on ABG (exclude haemolysis/spurious); continuous ECG — watch peaked T-waves, wide QRS.
  2. Stabilise the membrane: Ca gluconate 10% 0.5 mL/kg IV bolus (max 20 mL) — or CaCl₂ 10% 0.1–0.2 mL/kg. Repeat if ECG changes persist.
  3. Shift K⁺ intracellularly: Salbutamol neb / IV; NaHCO₃ 8.4% 1–2 mL/kg (or BE-based).
  4. Hyperventilate (once ETT-in-right-main-bronchus excluded).
  1. Insulin–Dextrose if not resolving (senior input):
    Emergency: 50% Dextrose 1 mL/kg + Insulin 0.1 U/kg.
    Gradual: 25% Dextrose 2 mL/kg/h until glucose >10 → Actrapid 0.05 U/kg/h.
  2. Salbutamol infusion: 5 mcg/kg/min ×1 h, then 1 mcg/kg/min.
  3. Diuresis: Furosemide 1 mg/kg. Monitor glucose & K⁺ regularly; aim glucose 10–15 mmol/L.
Calcium protects the myocardium but does NOT lower K⁺ — always follow with a shifting agent.

DOPES — Sudden Deterioration in an Intubated Child

AAGBI QRH · systematic
  1. Switch to 100% O₂; hand-ventilate off the machine with a self-inflating bag — removes ventilator/circuit as the cause and lets you feel compliance.
  2. D — Displacement: ETT too deep (endobronchial → unilateral chest rise), too shallow, or oesophageal. Check depth at lips, capnograph, auscultate; ultrasound / re-laryngoscopy if unsure.
  3. O — Obstruction: kink, secretions, mucus plug, blood, biting. Pass a suction catheter; if it won't pass, tube is blocked — remove & re-oxygenate.
  1. P — Pneumothorax: ↑airway pressure, ↓unilateral air entry, ↓SpO₂, hypotension. Tension → needle decompression / finger thoracostomy, then drain.
  2. E — Equipment: once bagging, work through circuit, valves, O₂ source, connectors, capnography line, ventilator settings. User error (wrong bag/mask size, wrong rate) is common in small children.
  3. S — Stacking & Stomach: Stacking (breath-stacking / gas trapping, esp. bronchospasm) → disconnect, allow long expiration. Stomach — gastric distension splints the diaphragm; decompress with NG/OG tube.
Disconnect and hand-ventilate first — if in doubt, take the machine out of the circuit.

Acute Desaturation + Loss of ETCO₂

AAGBI QRH · airway / circ
  1. Loss of ETCO₂ has two lethal causes — decide fast: (1) no circulation → cardiac arrest, or (2) no ventilation reaching the lungs → airway/circuit. Check a pulse now.
  2. No pulse → cardiac arrest algorithm (CPR, Adrenaline 10 mcg/kg).
  3. Pulse present → 100% O₂, hand-ventilate, run DOPES: displaced/obstructed ETT, disconnection, oesophageal, circuit obstruction.
  1. Confirm the airway: flat capnograph with an ETT = displacement, disconnection, or oesophageal → direct/video laryngoscopy to confirm cords.
  2. Also consider: severe bronchospasm, massive PE / air / fat embolism, low cardiac output — a sudden fall (not loss) of ETCO₂ with hypotension suggests embolism / falling output.
  3. Escalate; treat the cause; returning ETCO₂ is the marker of success.
A flat capnograph is either "no output" or "no ventilation" — a pulse check separates arrest from an airway problem. Waveform capnography is the key monitor (NAP4).

High Airway Pressure

AAGBI QRH
  1. 100% O₂; hand-ventilate with a self-inflating bag to feel compliance and separate circuit/equipment from patient causes.
  2. Equipment/circuit (relieved by disconnecting): obstructed / kinked ETT or circuit, stuck APL valve or ventilator, wrong settings, filter blockage. Easy to bag off the machine → fault is the machine/circuit.
  3. Patient — airway: run DOPES — displaced (endobronchial) or obstructed ETT (secretions, plug, biting), breath-stacking / gas trapping.
  1. Patient — lungs / chest: bronchospasm, tension pneumothorax (↓unilateral air entry, hypotension → decompress), pulmonary oedema, aspiration, light anaesthesia / straining.
  2. Surgical: retraction, pneumoperitoneum, position, surgeon leaning on the chest — communicate with the surgeon.
  3. Deepen ± relaxant if straining; treat the cause; recheck compliance.
Hand-ventilating is the fastest test — normal compliance off the machine means the fault is the circuit/ventilator, not the patient.

Machine / Ventilator Failure

AAGBI QRH
  1. Call for help; declare it. Maintain anaesthesia & oxygenation before fault-finding.
  2. Self-inflating bag + alternative O₂ (machine or separate cylinder). Hand-ventilate.
  3. Convert to TIVA (Propofol ± remi / Ketamine) — a stopped vaporiser risks awareness.
  1. Isolate fault: whole machine vs vaporiser / gas / ventilator / monitoring.
  2. Replacement machine; keep bag until two-bag check done. Full monitoring — SpO₂ + capnography.
  3. Document; report; remove faulty machine from service.
Self-inflating bag + O₂ cylinder keeps the patient alive independent of the machine. Start TIVA early.

O₂ Supply / Pipeline Failure

AAGBI QRH
  1. Declare; call for help. Suspect if O₂ analyser / pipeline gauge falls or low-O₂ alarm sounds.
  2. Open the O₂ cylinder on the machine — fastest independent supply.
  3. Disconnect the O₂ pipeline from the wall — a crossed pipeline can deliver a hypoxic mix; cylinder is only safe once wall supply is off.
  1. Self-inflating bag if machine flow unreliable. Minimise flows to conserve cylinder O₂.
  2. Maintain anaesthesia — TIVA if fresh gas / vaporiser compromised.
  3. Backup cylinders early; alert coordinator & estates; plan wake vs finish on reserve.
Disconnect the wall pipeline — a rising cylinder does not fix a crossed pipeline still feeding hypoxic gas.
